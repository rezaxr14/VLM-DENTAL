\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{No prospective clinician study.}
Our validity anchor is DENTEX's own dentist-verified ground truth (each image annotated by a
final-year dental student and verified by an expert dentist with over 15 years of experience)
plus automated reasoning-grounding checks by a cross-family LLM judge. This validates against
expert labels, not against a prospective, blinded review by practicing clinicians on new cases.
We state this plainly and consider a live reader study the natural next step for a team with
clinical access.

\paragraph{Training results are pending in this draft.}
The training-side evaluations (SFT, GRPO, ablations on the trained agent) are designed, frozen,
and implemented, but their runs are not complete at the time of writing; those cells are marked
as such rather than extrapolated. The completed evidence --- zero-shot baselines, grounding-tool
validation, and the corpus-level mechanism analysis --- stands independently, but the paper's
end-to-end claims await those runs.

\paragraph{Synthetic perturbations are not real detector errors.}
The tiered perturbation draws offsets from fixed configured ranges rather than from the
grounding model's actual error distribution. This is what makes demonstrations stable as the
detector improves (\cref{sec:perturbation}), but it means the training distribution of box
errors is synthetic: uniform random offsets with random signs, not the structured errors (e.g.,
adjacent-tooth confusions, root-tip truncations) a real detector exhibits. The evaluation-time
grounding-perturbation probe (\cref{sec:ablations}) measures robustness to a related synthetic
distribution; robustness to the detector's true error modes is best assessed post hoc by
swapping in improved detector weights, which our decoupling makes safe for the trace corpus but
does not substitute for measuring.

\paragraph{Demonstrated vs.\ learned correction.}
\cref{sec:mechanism} measures the \emph{teacher's} behavior in the demonstrations --- the skill
the student is trained to acquire --- not the trained policy's behavior at inference. The
policy-side version of the same analysis is precisely the grounding-perturbation probe, pending
the trained checkpoint. Demonstrated behavior is a necessary but not sufficient condition for
learned behavior.

\paragraph{Scale and scope.}
The corpus is 3{,}694 traces over two institutions and four DENTEX diagnosis classes (plus
Tufts' four-class taxonomy); it is anchored to precedents showing a few hundred verified
examples suffice for medical RL fine-tuning \citep{hu2025medvlmr1}, but it is small by
data-centric standards, and DENTEX's test set is 50 images --- confidence intervals on it are
wide, and we report them rather than over-reading point estimates. The tool suite covers
radiologist-like inspection primitives; measurement and calibration tools (lesion sizing,
periodontal probing analogues) and anomaly detection beyond the four classes are out of scope
for this paper.

\paragraph{Automated verification is not expert review.}
The verifier is a frontier model from a different family than the generator, audited against
expert labels --- and its rejection taxonomy shows it catches real failures --- but automated
verification has blind spots that human clinical review would not. We mitigate with
cross-family verification, ground-truth anchoring of every correctness claim, and release of
the full corpus and audit trail so that the community can re-audit it.
