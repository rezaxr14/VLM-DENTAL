\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our work touches four bodies of literature: supervised models for dental radiographs, reinforcement learning for
reasoning in medical vision-language models, tool-using agents in general and in dentistry, and reasoning traces
generated and verified by frontier models. We summarize each and say where we differ; the closest systems are the
concurrent dental agents discussed under agentic tool use.

\paragraph{Supervised dental AI.}
Dental radiograph analysis has been dominated by narrow, single-task supervised models: CNN
cephalometric landmark detectors \citep{arik2017cephalometry}, U-Net-style segmentation
\citep{ronneberger2015unet}, FDI tooth-numbering networks \citep{tuzoff2019tooth}, and
YOLO-family or Faster R-CNN detectors \citep{redmon2016yolo,ren2015faster} for caries,
periapical lesions, and impactions \citep{hamamci2024dentex}. The DENTEX benchmark consolidated these into a
hierarchical quadrant--enumeration--diagnosis task with expert-verified labels
\citep{hamamci2024dentex} and remains the most structured public benchmark for our problem.
These supervised tasks differ from our episode-level setting, in which an agent can request
additional views and revise a grounding hypothesis. A systematic review of public dental
imaging datasets \citep{uribe2024systematic} screened 131{,}028 records and found only 16
datasets that met a minimal bar, which motivates our synthetic-trace approach.

\paragraph{Reinforcement-learned reasoning in medical VLMs.}
Following the GRPO recipe \citep{deepseek2025r1}, \citet{pan2025medvlmr1} fine-tuned a 2B VLM
with GRPO on a few hundred examples and reported large gains over zero-shot across MRI, CT, and
X-ray VQA. Med-R1 extended this to eight modalities and found that longer chains of thought do
not reliably improve accuracy, so reward alignment matters more than reasoning length
\citep{wang2025medr1}. HuatuoGPT-o1 \citep{chen2024huatuo} and OpenMedReason
\citep{openmedreason2026} build reasoning supervision by using a frontier model as a
\emph{verifier} of evidence-grounded traces. These works motivate our training recipe; we add
the interaction between a reasoning agent and revisable visual observations.

\paragraph{Agentic tool use in VLMs.}
MMedAgent trains an LLM to select among grounding, segmentation, and report tools
\citep{li2024mmedagent}, and OctoTools generalizes the orchestration pattern
\citep{zhang2025octotools}. Closest in spirit is VTool-R1 \citep{wu2026vtool}, which trains a
VLM with GRPO to call Python-based visual-editing tools during reasoning and improves chart and
table QA from 64.7\% to 71.7\%. Its tools are generic image-editing primitives, its reward is a
single outcome-accuracy term, and its domain is not medical. Two concurrent dental systems are
closest to our work. \textbf{OralGPT-Plus} \citep{oralgptplus2026} combines instruction tuning on expert
trajectories, two callable image tools (zoom-in and contralateral mirror), and GRPO with a hybrid reward
whose accuracy term is a rubric score from an LLM judge. It evaluates on its own MMOral-X benchmark and on
MMOral-OPG \citep{hao2025mmoral}, and reports that without its conditioned reward term, training collapses toward
invoking a single tool and answering without inspection. It ablates its training stages, reward terms, and
mirror tool, but to our knowledge has no paired no-tools arm trained with the same recipe, so the effect of tool
use itself is not isolated. \textbf{OralAgent} \citep{oralagent2026} is a broader prompted ReAct orchestrator
around an off-the-shelf model with 22 tools and retrieval over dental textbooks, not an RL-trained tool-use
policy; it evaluates on MMOral-Uni, MMOral-OPG, and OralQA-ZH. A third system, OPGAgent
\citep{yu2026opgagent}, likewise plans with a prompted model over a toolbox of detectors and dental vision experts,
and evaluates on its own OPG-Bench and MMOral-OPG. Our contribution is a box-relative corrective action and a
controlled evaluation of tools and RL; we do not claim to originate dental agents or medical tool use.
Comparisons should account for differences in datasets, available supervision, and scoring protocols.

\paragraph{Procedural context and speculative serving.}
The rules-and-skills extension follows the inspectable \texttt{SKILL.md} convention with
progressive disclosure \citep{agentskills2026}; it contributes a provenance-tracked integration
with the shared dental dispatcher, not a new knowledge-file format. Speculative decoding uses a
draft proposal and target verification to accelerate generation
\citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating}, and vLLM provides the serving substrate
\citep{kwon2023efficient}. We treat serving speed and clinical-task performance as separate
measurements and test output equivalence on the actual multimodal episodes instead of assuming
it from the algorithm.

\paragraph{Label-anchored synthetic reasoning traces.}
Trace generation of this kind replaces prospective expert annotation with expert-\emph{verified benchmark
labels} plus cross-model automated checks. We extend the pattern to \emph{multi-turn tool-use} traces: the demonstrator operates a real tool suite under
real tool feedback, including deliberately imperfect tool output (\cref{sec:perturbation}), and
an independent cross-family verifier audits every trace against expert labels before it enters
the training set.
