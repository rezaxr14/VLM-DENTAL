\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dental panoramic radiographs (orthopantomograms, OPGs) are the workhorse imaging modality of
general dentistry, and annotating them --- \emph{which tooth, and what is wrong with it}, in
standard FDI notation --- is the task that ultimately feeds treatment planning. It is also a task
on which general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) visibly fail. Frontier multimodal models
evaluate at best 36--46\% exact-match accuracy against expert labels on the DENTEX benchmark and
carry expected calibration errors of up to 0.68, reporting 0.85--0.95 confidence while
hallucinating tooth positions (\cref{sec:baselines}); a dense 11B generalist collapses below the
statistical majority-class floor. The failure is not primarily a knowledge gap. An OPG is a
single very wide image in which an early interproximal carious lesion occupies fewer than
$15\times15$ native pixels --- reduced to a sub-patch blur by the vision encoder's resolution
budget --- and in which left/right inversion conventions routinely flip quadrant labels. What the
task rewards is exactly what a radiologist does and a single-pass VLM cannot: \emph{look again}
--- zoom into a suspect region, adjust contrast, compare against the contralateral jaw half, and
verify an anatomical hypothesis before committing to it.

A growing body of work equips VLMs with callable tools to close this gap
\citep{wu2026vtool,li2024mmedagent,zhang2025octotools}, and two concurrent systems do so for
dentistry specifically \citep{oralgptplus2026,oralagent2026}. But in all of them the tools the
model calls are \emph{trusted} operations: a zoom returns a crop, a mirror returns a composite,
and the model has no way to tell the tool that its output is wrong. This is a structural
mismatch with the medical setting, where the most informative component the system can call ---
a learned grounding model that says ``tooth 36 is here'' --- is precisely the component whose
errors the agent must anticipate rather than inherit. A grounding box that is off by half a
tooth-width does not merely lose accuracy; it silently poisons every downstream observation,
because the crop looks authoritative.

This paper develops and evaluates \sys{}, an agentic VLM for OPG diagnosis built around a
different contract between the policy and its tools: \emph{grounding is a hypothesis, not an
answer}. \cref{fig:architecture} gives the complete system architecture, and \cref{fig:traces}
contrasts the two trace arms on one image. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{A self-correcting grounding tool (\texttt{nudge\_crop}, \cref{sec:nudge}).}
The policy can shift and rescale a box it was \emph{already given} --- by the grounding model or
by a previous correction --- using box-relative fractional arguments (\texttt{dx\_frac},
\texttt{dy\_frac}, \texttt{scale}) that a VLM can reason about linguistically
(``shift right about half a tooth-width''), without re-running detection. To our knowledge
neither concurrent dental agentic system \citep{oralgptplus2026,oralagent2026} offers the policy
any mechanism to correct a specialist tool's own uncertainty.
\item \textbf{A tiered perturbation mechanism that makes self-correction learnable
(\cref{sec:perturbation}).} Demonstrating correction behavior requires the demonstrator to
\emph{experience} tool error. During trace generation we return the expert-verified box for
annotated teeth (guaranteeing task solvability) but deliberately perturb the box
\emph{shown to the demonstrator} --- 45\% clean, 25\% small offsets (12--28\% of box size), 30\%
large offsets (45--75\%) --- with two design commitments: the rates are fixed constants decoupled
from the detector's real error rate, so demonstrations never go stale as the detector improves;
and errors are never announced verbally, so the offset magnitude alone must carry the signal and
the learned behavior survives to reinforcement learning and inference time, where no such cue
exists. A corpus-level analysis (\cref{sec:mechanism}) shows the mechanism elicits exactly the
intended behavior gradient in 3{,}694 generated demonstrations: corrective use of
\texttt{nudge\_crop} on 38.4\% of large offsets, 13.6\% of small ones (a genuine judgment call),
and 1.1\% of clean boxes, on ground-truth-anchored pathology locates.
\item \textbf{A tool interface with continuous parameters (\cref{sec:tools}).} Tools expose
\emph{how much} effect to apply, not just which tool to call: \texttt{denoise(strength
$\in[0,1]$)}, \texttt{window\_level(center, width)} as an override on top of presets,
\texttt{enhance\_contrast(factor)}, \texttt{zoom\_crop(padding\_frac)}. Tool calls therefore
carry real information beyond tool identity, and the policy can match processing intensity to
image characteristics.
\item \textbf{A ground-truth-anchored, correction-friendly reward (\cref{sec:reward}).} GRPO
training \citep{deepseek2025r1} uses a composite reward whose dominant term is a hard,
non-LLM-judge match against expert labels with multi-finding greedy one-to-one matching
(F1-style harmonic mean of recall and precision), and whose efficiency term scales a reference
budget with case complexity and \emph{exempts} \texttt{locate\_tooth} and \texttt{nudge\_crop}
from per-call cost --- so the reward never prices out the verification behavior it is trying to
teach, a failure mode we identified and removed by design.
\item \textbf{A fully synthetic, clinician-free data pipeline and its audit trail
(\cref{sec:traces}).} 3{,}694 verified chain-of-thought tool-use traces over DENTEX and Tufts
(1{,}847 with tools, 1{,}847 single-turn without, exact 1:1 parity), generated by frontier models
under real tool execution in a LangGraph loop, verified cross-family, decontaminated of
directive leakage (105 traces regenerated; 0 leaks in the canonical corpus), and released as a
reusable resource. The verifier's rejection taxonomy (66 false-positive findings on healthy
scans, 15 ground-truth contradictions, 11 uncorrected spatial drifts) doubles as an audit of
what the perturbation mechanism does and does not elicit.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate on DENTEX with its native structured metrics --- pooled, set-matched,
finding-level precision/recall/F1 with paired bootstrap intervals --- against zero-shot frontier
VLMs, the same backbone with and without tools, and a supervised specialist detector. The
training-side analyses in this draft are complete; the two-stage training runs (SFT, then GRPO)
are in progress, and their result cells are marked \todo{SFT/GRPO results pending first
hardware run} rather than extrapolated. The zero-shot baseline table, the grounding-tool
validation, and the corpus-level mechanism analysis are final.
