\begin{abstract}
Panoramic dental diagnosis requires both identifying a finding and assigning it to the
correct tooth. In our zero-shot DENTEX evaluation, the strongest reported baseline obtains
pooled exact finding-level F1 of 0.222, motivating methods that can revisit localized image
evidence. We present \textbf{VLM-DENTAL}, a tool-using vision-language pipeline organized around
one principle: \emph{grounding is a hypothesis to interrogate, not an answer to trust}.
An eight-tool interface lets a policy inspect native-resolution regions and correct a proposed
box using box-relative shifts and scales through \texttt{nudge\_crop}. During demonstration
synthesis, tiered spatial perturbations expose the teacher to imperfect grounding without
verbal error cues. A ground-truth-anchored reward is designed to preserve corrective tool use
during subsequent reinforcement learning. The released corpus contains 3{,}694 verified,
decontaminated synthetic traces. Within ground-truth-anchored pathology locates, the teacher
uses corrective nudges on 38.4\% of large offsets, 13.6\% of small offsets, and 1.1\% of clean
outputs; these are demonstration statistics, not trained-policy results. We additionally
specify a versioned rules-and-skills harness and a separately gated speculative-decoding
benchmark, keeping inference-time procedural context distinct from model training and serving
optimizations. The frozen primary evaluation uses DENTEX-native set matching and paired
image-level bootstrap intervals. Trained-policy, harness-effectiveness, and serving-speed
results remain explicitly pending. This draft contributes an auditable method, corpus, and
experimental specification; evidence of end-to-end diagnostic improvement awaits those runs.
\end{abstract}
