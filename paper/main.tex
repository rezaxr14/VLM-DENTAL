\begin{abstract}
Panoramic dental diagnosis requires both identifying a finding and assigning it to the
correct tooth. In our zero-shot evaluation on DENTEX, the strongest baseline reaches a pooled
exact finding-level F1 of only 0.222, which motivates methods that can revisit localized image
evidence. We present \textbf{VLM-DENTAL}, a tool-using vision-language pipeline built on one
principle: \emph{grounding is a hypothesis to interrogate, not an answer to trust}.
An eight-tool interface lets a policy inspect native-resolution regions and revise a proposed
box through box-relative shifts and scales (\texttt{nudge\_crop}). During demonstration
synthesis, tiered spatial perturbations expose the teacher to imperfect grounding without
verbal error cues, and a ground-truth-anchored reward is designed to preserve corrective tool
use during reinforcement learning. The released corpus contains 3{,}694 verified,
decontaminated synthetic traces. On ground-truth-anchored pathology locates, the teacher
issues corrective nudges on 38.4\% of large offsets, 13.6\% of small offsets, and 1.1\% of
clean boxes; these are demonstration statistics, not trained-policy results. We further
specify a versioned rules-and-skills harness and a separately gated speculative-decoding
benchmark, which keep inference-time procedural context distinct from model training and
serving optimization. Evaluation follows a frozen protocol with DENTEX-native set matching and
paired image-level bootstrap intervals; trained-policy, harness, and serving results are
pending and are not claimed.
\end{abstract}
